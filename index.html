Please Read This Before Uploading Data ▼
  • Required CSV Columns: A header row with at least Customer ID, Order Date and Unit Price (or a total Amount). Order ID and Quantity are optional: if your file has no order number, each row counts as one order; if it has no quantity, quantity is taken as 1. Each row should represent one purchase line.
  • Check the Column Mapping: After uploading, a "Map CSV Header Columns Correctly" section appears. Make sure each field points to the correct column in your file (e.g., Order Date → your date column, Order ID → your invoice/order number). Each of the 5 fields needs a different column. If two fields share the same column, the analysis will not run.
  • Date Format: Most formats work (e.g. 2026-06-30, 30/06/2026, 06/30/2026). Use "Data Settings" if day/month order is unclear. Rows with unreadable dates are dropped.
  • Currency & Refunds: Set your currency symbol in "Data Settings". Rows with negative amounts are ignored unless you tick the refunds option.
  • Missing Data: Automatically drops incomplete invoice rows, including rows with a price or quantity of zero or below.
How RFM Metrics & Feature Scaling Are Calculated ▼

To cluster customers accurately, raw transaction rows are aggregated per unique customer ID and transformed using the following mathematical pipeline:

1. Recency (R)

Days since last purchase relative to max date in file.

R = (MaxDate + 1) - LastOrderDate
2. Frequency (F)

Total count of distinct invoice numbers per customer.

F = CountDistinct(OrderID)
3. Monetary (M)

Cumulative revenue generated across all transactions.

M = Sum(UnitPrice × Quantity)
Why Log Transformation & Z-Score Scaling Are Essential

A. Log Transformation: E-commerce spending has extreme high-end outliers. Applying log(1 + x) compresses long-tailed distributions into a normal curve.

B. Z-Score Standardization: Since Monetary spend ranges in thousands ($) while Frequency ranges from 1 to 10, spend would artificially dominate K-Means. We scale all metrics to Mean = 0 and StdDev = 1 using: Z = (X - Mean) / StdDev.

4. Clusters & segment names

Clusters (K): K-means groups customers with similar behaviour. The possible range of K and the recommended K are worked out from your data size and the silhouette score, so they change with every dataset.

Segment names: each cluster is described by how its average recency, frequency and spend compare with all customers in your file (above or below average, measured in standard deviations). Clusters are ranked by overall value, and no fixed business profiles are used.
